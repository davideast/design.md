The interface is a physical object: a cardstock J-card folded into a plastic case. It feels domestic, warm, and slightly fragile. The layout follows the logic of a paper insert—creased lines serve as dividers, and information is "printed" rather than rendered. The experience is about the friction of the physical: you don't just tap a track, you "engage" the player.

The visual language avoids neon "vaporwave" tropes in favor of the tactile reality of home recording. Palette is dominated by unbleached paper and magnetic tape oxide, with accents of dried ballpoint pen ink and translucent highlighter marks. Type mimics the low-fidelity output of 90s home printers and the friendly, bulbous industrial design of consumer electronics from that decade.
