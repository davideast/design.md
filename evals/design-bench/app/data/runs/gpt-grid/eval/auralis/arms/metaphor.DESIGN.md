## Overview

This page embodies a single governing idea.

## Method

The page is a recording studio, and the visitor has just stepped inside. A studio is the quietest room ever built — pale, soft-edged, acoustically treated — and everything in it exists so that one thing can happen at its center: a voice, made visible. The page at large is the treated room: warm off-white walls {colors.neutral}, white acoustic modules {colors.surface-elevated} seated with hairline seams {colors.outline-muted}, text in calm ink {colors.primary} with engineering notes in grey {colors.secondary}. Treatment has no hard edges — foam, fabric, capsules — so every surface curves {rounded.lg} and every control is a pill {rounded.full}, the soft hardware of a console. A treated room has no echo, so nothing casts a shadow; surfaces meet by seam and tone, except the one monitor that may sit with the faintest footprint beneath it. The live room is the demo panel: behind its glass, the voice itself appears — a vivid bloom of purple {colors.tertiary}, magenta, cerulean, and pink suspended in light, the way sound would look if you could see it hanging in air — luminous, organic, the only color in the building, with a single white capsule button at its center like a talkback switch. The control room is the dark dashboard: the one dim space {colors.primary} in the studio, all meters, waveform, and transport, lit by its own readouts. The signage is studio signage: one typeface throughout {typography.h1}, terse tracked-caps labels on every door {typography.label-caps} — AURALIS on the front plate — and short engraved glyphs beside each control, never illustrations. The hero is the soundproof door: a vast quiet wall holding one sentence {typography.h1} and two pill switches, with the silence around it doing the talking. In a studio nothing is decorative; everything either makes the room quieter or makes the voice clearer.

## Colors

The room: walls {colors.neutral}, modules {colors.surface-elevated}, seams {colors.outline-muted}, ink {colors.primary}, engineering grey {colors.secondary}; and the voice {colors.tertiary} with its spectral blues and pinks, visible only behind the glass.

## Typography

Studio signage: {typography.h1} and {typography.h2} set tight and dense, {typography.body-md} for the engineer's notes, {typography.label-caps} for door plates and meters, {typography.button} on the console switches.

## Layout

The architecture of quiet: generous treated space between sections, modules on an exact grid, the live room given the widest sightline, the control room full-width and dim.
