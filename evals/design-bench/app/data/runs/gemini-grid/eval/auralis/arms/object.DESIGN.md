## Overview

The Auralis page is presented as a precision instrument, not a marketing site.

## Method

This page is a flagship laboratory audio instrument from a Scandinavian acoustics firm — the measurement microphone, the reference monitor, the analyzer that costs more than a car — photographed for its own product literature on a seamless white table. The chassis is matte off-white {colors.neutral}; the engraved markings are dense near-black {colors.primary} set in one engineered grotesk, tight and exact; the fine calibration text {colors.secondary} recedes the way etched secondary scales do. Every control on such an instrument is a machined capsule — pill-shaped buttons {rounded.full}, soft to the thumb, exact to the grid — and every panel is a smooth rounded module {rounded.lg} seated flush into the chassis with a hairline seam {colors.outline-muted}, never a floating card with a shadow; precision equipment does not glow or hover, it sits. Its labels are stamped specimen-style — small caps, wide tracking {typography.label-caps}, the wordmark engraved as AURALIS the way a serial plate reads — and its glyph language is a single engraved icon set, uniform in weight, one symbol per function. The instrument is quiet everywhere except one place: its display. Where the machine renders sound, color blooms — a vivid, organic cloud of deep purple {colors.tertiary} and magenta bleeding into cerulean and cyan with pink at the edges, like pigment dropped into still water on the white table — luminous and airy, the one living thing on a body of disciplined calm. That display is the page's demo panel; the dark dashboard is the instrument's rear service panel, the one near-black module {colors.primary} on the device. Everything the brochure photographs is lit flat and even: at most one soft, barely-there shadow under the mounted instrument itself, and none anywhere else.

## Colors

Chassis {colors.neutral} and module white {colors.surface-elevated}, engraved ink {colors.primary}, calibration grey {colors.secondary}, hairline seams {colors.outline-muted}, and the display's spectral purple {colors.tertiary} — color only where the instrument shows sound.

## Typography

One engineered grotesk at every scale: {typography.h1} and {typography.h2} tight as engraved faceplates, {typography.body-md} for the manual's prose, {typography.label-caps} for stamped labels and the AURALIS plate, {typography.button} for control captions.

## Layout

The instrument's grid: modules seated on an exact 12-column chassis, generous machined margins, controls aligned to the millimeter, one display given pride of place.
