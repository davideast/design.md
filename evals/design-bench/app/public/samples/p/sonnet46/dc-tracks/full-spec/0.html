Georgetown Waterfront Ward 2

Where the Potomac Keeps the Pace

Georgetown Waterfront's oval sits precisely at the junction of the C&O Canal and the Potomac, and at dusk the water doubles the sky so completely that a runner on the back straight feels momentarily suspended between two horizons. The surface is fresh tartan — springy, consistent, maintained with the care that proximity to affluence permits.

Regulars: scullers before the water warms, Hill staffers on compressed lunch hours, and the solitary who come after midnight when the towpath empties and the lane lines glow faint orange under sodium lamps.

Anacostia Park Ward 8

The Oldest Oval East of the River

Anacostia Park's track carries decades in its surface — rubberized asphalt that absorbs the morning heat by ten and holds it well past evening. The infield has hosted little league seasons, community rallies, and the steady patience of the same group of elderly walkers who have circled every Tuesday since 1987.

Open without fee or gate, it remains the most democratic of DC's ovals. The Anacostia River is close enough to hear on windless nights, and Reagan's flight paths mark the laps in 737 shadow intervals — the city's most honest split timer.
