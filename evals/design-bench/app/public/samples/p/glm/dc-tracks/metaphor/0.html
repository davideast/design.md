◆ Georgetown Waterfront Track

Where the river paces the runner.

The oval sits low against the Potomac, half a metre above the waterline on a still morning, so close that scullers call out their splits to the regulars in Lane 4. Built on the footprint of an old freight pier, the surface runs soft in the rain — a mercy for aging knees, a frustration for anyone chasing a personal record.

Most evenings the inner lanes belong to Georgetown's varsity; the outer lanes belong to the city, and the city runs them well into November.

◆ Anacostia Park Track

A slower oval, set against a wilder river.

East of the river, the track opens onto a flat horizon of field and cottonwood. The surface is older here — a poured latex, weathered silver at the curves, kind to no one and honest with everyone. The wind comes unbroken off the Anacostia and the back straight is where it finds you.

On Saturday mornings the local club runs intervals in unison, their footfalls marking time like a single organism turning left.

Distance 400 m
Surface Latex · Weathered
