From the Source

Twice a year I pack a single bag and a notebook and go to where the leaf is grown. In the spring I fly to Taiwan, to the narrow mountain lanes above Beipu, where Mrs. Tsai walks me through rows of Oriental Beauty bushes and shows me the white leafhopper bites that give the tea its honey. We taste the new harvest in small porcelain cups, the steep still warm from the kettle.

In the autumn I travel to Kenya, to the high ridges above Kericho, where the tea fields roll in long green lines under red earth. The pickers work before the heat sets in, and by mid-morning we are sorting leaf in the withering loft, the air thick with the smell of bruised green leaf.
