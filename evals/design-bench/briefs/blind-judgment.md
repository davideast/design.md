# Brief — blind judgment

Design a desktop web page where the reader judges renderings without knowing which treatment produced them. The structure: several unlabeled results from the same brief, one verdict, then a reveal (the reveal is a separate page, briefed separately). This brief carries content only — all visual direction comes from the project's DESIGN.md.

The page contains, in sequence:

## 1. Wayfinding and framing

A link back to "Agent Architecture," and the frame in two sentences: "Below are five renderings of the same seven-slide deck — all from Stitch · Gemini 3.1 Pro, in shuffled order, with their treatments hidden. Pick the one with the clearest point of view — then see which method made it." A small companion link offers the other blind test: "Judge tools instead — same direction, the tool hidden."

## 2. The five unlabeled renderings

Five results at equal size (use placeholder images), labeled only by neutral letters A through E. No treatment names, no distances, no captions beyond the letter. Each is selectable as the reader's verdict.

## 3. The verdict

A single question with the five options: "Which rendering most escapes the look you'd expect by default?" and a confirm action reading "Lock in and reveal." One quiet line beneath: "Your verdict is recorded before the reveal — it can't be changed after."

## 4. Progress

A small indication of where the reader stands in the sequence: "Judgment 2 of 6 in this set."
