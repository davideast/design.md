## Overview

The home page should feel warm, unhurried, and neighborly — a quiet welcome you could lean into rather than a storefront shouting for attention.

## Method

The mood is gentle, settled, and homemade — handmade without being precious. Everything should read as calm, generous, and patient: soft, low, and quietly confident. The page is friendly but never chatty, plain but never bare, modest but visibly cared for. It should feel the way steam feels — light, slow, rising, rounded at the edges, never sharp and never hurried. Warmth comes first: a low golden warmth, the warmth of a small room with the kettle on and the windows fogged. The voice is honest and a little unpolished, artisanal in the truest sense — the work of one person who knows the regulars by name and remembers how they take it. Nothing here should feel glossy, corporate, or loud; aim instead for the soft-spoken, the handwritten, the lived-in. Restful, airy, breathable, sincere. The three teas should feel celebrated yet understated, each given its own small dignity without fuss. ===FILE arms/constraint.DESIGN.md===
## Overview

Every choice on this page is deduced from one fact: it must reproduce as a single two-ink risograph pull on uncoated paper.

## Method

A risograph lays flat soybean ink through a drum onto rough stock {colors.neutral}, one drum per pass. It cannot hold a soft shadow — the screen would band and break — so no element on this page casts one; depth is impossible, and the page lies flat on the paper instead of floating above it. It cannot saturate a dark field without the ink bleeding through and ghosting the sheet, so there are no dark panels anywhere; large areas stay paper, and structure is made from blank space and the occasional thin rule. The machine gives me exactly two drums. The first is the key, {colors.primary}, and it carries all the type and every line. Where I want the quieter grey {colors.secondary} — the hours table, the address, a hairline — I do not add a third ink; I under-ink the key drum to a screen, the only grey the press can make. The second drum is the single warm accent {colors.tertiary}, and because a second pass means re-feeding every sheet and trusting loose registration, it earns its cost only by appearing rarely and never touching the key — one band beneath the shop name, one mark beside today's date, set with enough air around it that a half-millimetre of misregistration reads as intention rather than error. Fine reversed type would fill in on the rough stock, so all type sits positive, ink on paper, at honest weights. The result is forced, not styled: flat, warm, and plain because the press allows nothing else — and because nothing on the page could survive a second pull that didn't.

## Colors

The two drums: key ink {colors.primary} on paper {colors.neutral}, the key screened back to {colors.secondary} for tables and rules, and the second-drum accent {colors.tertiary} pressed once or twice where it is worth re-feeding the sheet.

## Typography

What the drum holds clean: {typography.headline-lg} for the name and welcome, {typography.body-md} for the teas and the sourcing essay, {typography.label-sm} for the hours table, the address, and the footer — all positive, all flat ink.

## Layout

A single sheet, fed once: wide margins the grippers need, content inset and breathing on open paper, separation built from white space and screened hairlines because that is all a clean pull will keep.
===END===

## Overview

The page is a clear glass of tea held up to a window — colour suspended in light, with nothing behind it.

## Method

Steeping is the governing idea, and every element maps onto it. A glass held to the light has no dark side and casts no shadow into itself: light passes straight through, so the page is lit, not lit-from-above — flat warm paper {colors.neutral} with no element floating over another, because in clear water nothing hovers and nothing falls into shade. The colour in the glass is the leaf releasing into the water, and that is the one warm accent {colors.tertiary}: it belongs only to the act of brewing, so it appears where the brew is — the bloom of colour beneath the shop name, the single mark at today's date, the first letter of each tea — and nowhere structural, because tinting the frame would mean colour where no leaf is steeping. The three brewed teas are three steeps in a tasting flight, set side by side at equal strength in {typography.body-md} so the eye can read one against the next, none deeper or louder than the others. The owner's sourcing paragraph is the water itself — the medium everything dissolves into — a calm clear block of prose the colour of plain ink {colors.primary}. The hours, address, and phone are sediment settling at the bottom of the glass: fine, quiet, true, ranked in {typography.label-sm} along the foot where settled things come to rest. And darkness is over-steeping — the brew gone bitter and opaque — so it has no place here; the glass stays clear, the light stays in it, and what isn't suspended in that light simply isn't on the page.

## Colors

Light through the glass: paper {colors.neutral} as the lit field, plain ink {colors.primary} as the water, settled grey {colors.secondary} for the sediment of rules and tables, and the leaf's colour {colors.tertiary} only where something is steeping.

## Typography

{typography.headline-lg} for the name, the welcome poured beneath it; {typography.body-md} for the three steeps and the water of the essay; {typography.label-sm} for the sediment — hours, address, phone — at the foot.

## Layout

A tall clear glass: an open lit field with wide margins, the three steeps ranked level across the middle, the essay as the body of water around them, and the settled credits resting along the bottom edge.

## Overview

A complete specification for the Kettle & Leaf home page, grounded in the reverse of a 1959 Blue Note LP jacket: a warm, flat, text-led broadside that reads as a printed object lying on a counter rather than a screen.

## Method

The page is the liner side of a Reid Miles–era Blue Note jacket, re-warmed and brought to tea. Bold-weighted Franklin Gothic crowds a warm paper field; the shop name and welcome are the album title and tagline; the three brewed teas are the track listing, each name set bold with origin and tasting note trailing as a credit; the owner's sourcing paragraph is the liner essay set as one confident column; the quiet-hours table and address are catalogue data; the hours and phone are the ruled credits block at the foot. Where a Blue Note front is dark, cool, and photographic, this design takes the *back* and warms it: paper field, positive ink, square printed corners, and a single warm spot in place of the cool duotone. The page lies flat on its surface — there is no element raised above another, and the only depth is the depth of reading order.

## Colors

- `{colors.neutral}` — the jacket paper; the full field and the only background anywhere.
- `{colors.primary}` — liner ink; all primary type, all rules, all tea names.
- `{colors.secondary}` — catalogue grey; the hours table, the address line, recording-date metadata, hairline rules.
- `{colors.tertiary}` — the warm spot, used at most twice on the page: a rule beneath the title and a single mark at today's date. Never as a fill, never behind type.

## Typography

- **Title role** — `{typography.headline-lg}`: the shop name and one-line welcome, top of the jacket.
- **Listing & essay role** — `{typography.body-md}`, bold weight for tea names and lead-ins, regular for tasting notes and the sourcing essay.
- **Credits role** — `{typography.label-sm}`: the quiet-hours table, the address, and the footer hours and phone, ranked and slightly tracked like ruled credits.

## Layout

- Corners are `{rounded.none}` throughout — printed card, not soft UI.
- Outer margin `{spacing.xl}`; the title clears the listing by `{spacing.lg}`; lines within a tea by `{spacing.sm}`; between teas `{spacing.md}`.
- A wide warm field: title across the top, the three-tea listing column and the liner essay sharing the middle band, the ruled credits block — hours, phone, address — pinned along the foot above a single hairline.
- Structure is made from rules and blank paper, never from raised surfaces.

## Do's and Don'ts

- **Do** keep every surface the paper field `{colors.neutral}`; let separation come from rules and white space.
- **Do** set all type positive in `{colors.primary}` at honest weights, ink on paper.
- **Do** spend `{colors.tertiary}` exactly twice, as a line and a mark — never as a panel or a button.
- **Do** square every corner and let the page sit flat on its surface.
- **Don't** raise any element above the field or imply depth between blocks.
- **Don't** reverse type out of a dark panel or place any block on a filled background.
- **Don't** introduce a second accent, a tint behind text, or a photographic hero.
