## Overview
The Pocket Tides interface is modeled after the Nikonos III, a legendary 1960s underwater camera known for its indestructible metal build and high-contrast mechanical controls. The design adopts this "sea-hardened" persona, treating every digital element as a physical, precision-engineered component.

## Method
Like the camera’s heavy-duty chassis, the UI is constructed with a sense of weight and mechanical permanence. Modules are framed with thick, definitive borders that mimic the Nikonos’s gasketed seals. Data is presented not as "content" but as "readings" etched into the surface of a tool. The design avoids soft edges and subtle transitions, favoring the sharp, tactile clarity of a mechanical depth scale. Every interaction point feels like a knurled metal knob or a heavy-duty toggle, emphasizing a "tool-in-hand" experience for the surfer on the beach.

## Colors
The color scheme draws from the camera’s industrial materials. {colors.primary} serves as the "black-anodized" metal body of the interface, providing the main structural weight. {colors.neutral} acts as the high-contrast "engraved" paint within the markings, ensuring maximum legibility. {colors.secondary} is used for the "brushed steel" components of the charts and tables. {colors.tertiary} is applied as the "safety orange" found on the camera’s critical indicators, drawing the eye to the most vital surf conditions.

## Typography
Type is treated as mechanical engraving. {typography.headline-lg} is used for the primary tide levels, mimicking the bold, stamped numbers on the camera’s lens barrel. {typography.body-md} provides the paddle-out instructions with the utility of a technical manual. {typography.label-sm} is used for all secondary data, its monospaced layout mirroring the precise, technical increments of a shutter speed dial or a distance scale.

## Layout
The layout is a series of stacked, "machined" compartments. The tide visualization is framed like a viewfinder window, with the curve rendered as a sharp, etched line. Data tables for the 5-day outlook are organized in tight, functional rows that look like a depth-of-field chart. Elements are packed closely with a focus on durability and density, ensuring the information feels contained and protected from the elements.
