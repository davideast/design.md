## Overview
The interface is a **Static Taxonomic Map**, a document where every sighting and species is a fixed coordinate in a landscape of data. It treats information as a physical geography to be navigated with a magnifying glass.

## Method
In this world, the screen is an **Analytical Specimen Sheet**. Every species isn't just an entry in a list; it is a specimen to be mapped. The "Pointer System"—using sharp {colors.accent} arrows—functions like a scientist’s pins in a display box, calling out specific morphological details. The relationship between elements is spatial and hierarchical rather than temporal. The "Urban Filter" from the field notes is visualized as a subtraction of layers on a map, stripping away noise to reveal the core signal of the bird. The interface doesn't "react" with modern animations; it simply reveals itself as a complete, authoritative diagram.

## Colors
The colors represent the layers of the map. {colors.background} is the base terrain—the paper field where the study takes place. {colors.text} represents the inked lines of the map’s grid and its primary legends. {colors.ui-neutral} indicates the "noise" or the secondary features—the urban background that must be filtered out. {colors.accent} is the "signal"—the specific, critical identification mark that the observer is searching for in the dark.

## Typography
The typography functions as the map’s labeling system. {typography.headings} and {typography.labels} are the large-scale markers—the "continents" and "cities" of the birding guide. {typography.body} is the "fine print," the detailed terrain data that provides context and scientific depth to each sighting. The typeface choices reinforce the feeling of a technical document that has been professionally typeset for archival purposes.

## Layout
The layout is a rigid coordinate system. Every piece of information is assigned a cell within a dense, unyielding grid, separated by sharp 1pt rules. There are no rounded corners because the map is a document of straight lines and precise measurements. Illustrations are silhouettes that act as "icons" on the map, providing a quick visual reference for the species' form without the distraction of texture or color.
