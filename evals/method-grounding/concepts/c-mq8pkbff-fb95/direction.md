# The Peterson 1960s Field Guide

This direction is a mid-century scientific manual designed for the pocket. It avoids the "slickness" of digital interfaces in favor of the physical constraints of 1960s offset printing on matte paper stock.

### The Visual Language
The page is defined by rigid, thin black keylines and a total lack of photography. Instead, the world is represented through clean, diagrammatic illustrations. The most critical grounding element is the **Pointer System**: small, sharp red arrows (Pointer Red) that physically call out specific identifying features within the illustrations or text blocks. 

### Materiality and Layout
- **The Grid:** Dense and utilitarian. Information is packed tightly as if to save paper, but remains highly legible.
- **Chrome:** No gradients, shadows, or rounded corners. Buttons and inputs are simple boxes defined by 1pt strokes.
- **Imagery:** High-contrast line art. Birds are shown in profile or "in-flight" silhouettes, emphasizing form over texture.

### Register
Scientific, instructional, and durable. It should feel like a tool that has been carried through the city in a jacket pocket—functional, authoritative, and slightly weathered by use. It rejects the "app" aesthetic entirely, behaving instead like a digitized scan of a physical guide.
