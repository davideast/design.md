# Brief — home: the case gallery

Design a desktop web page that is the front door of the tool. The reader is a first-time visitor who has not seen an evaluation case before; the page's job is to let them pick a case and understand in one screen what this tool demonstrates. This brief carries content only — all visual direction comes from the project's DESIGN.md.

The page contains, in sequence:

## 1. Identity

The tool's name, "Design Bench," and one sentence stating what it is: "A bench for testing how AI design tools render the same brief — the same content under different design directions and different tools, measured and compared."

## 2. The cases

Four cases, each presented as an entry the reader can open. Each entry contains: the case name, a one-line statement of its content world, a small image area showing its strongest rendering (use a placeholder image), and a one-line result stated as a claim — naming the tool that produced it, since a result always belongs to a tool. The four:

1. **Agent Architecture** — "A seven-slide technical talk on AI agent loops. Naming one real object — a graduate CS lecture handout — moved results 0.64 farther from the default look than adjectives did, on Stitch · Gemini 3.1 Pro."
2. **Auralis** — "A marketing site for an AI voice platform. The object grounding outperformed adjectives by 0.36 across 18 renderings, on Stitch · Gemini 3.1 Pro."
3. **DC Tracks** — "An editorial music publication for Washington, DC. The metaphor grounding led adjectives by 0.21 across 42 renderings, on Stitch · Gemini 3.1 Pro."
4. **Urban Nocturnal Field Guide** — "A field-notes site for night birding in the city, designed as a mid-century scientific manual. Newest case; measurement in progress."

## 3. How to read the evidence

A short section defining the terms the case pages use, in plain words: "A *treatment* is what the model was given — from nothing at all up to a complete specification. The *default look* is what a tool produces with no direction; every distance on this site is measured from it. *Adjectives* are the treatment being challenged: the common practice of describing a design in words like clean, modern, and bold. A case can also be compared across *tools* — the same direction handed to several — scored by *fidelity*, how faithfully each reproduced it."

## 4. Paths for makers

Two quieter links for people who build cases rather than read them: "Start a new case from a spark" and "All measurements" — with one phrase each: "an ideation partner helps you find the world before any files exist" and "every batch, model, and definition hash."
