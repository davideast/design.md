## object

**A hand-brushed tea card from a 1955 Kyoto kissaten, ink on a single sheet of kozo washi**
This carries a vertical, columnar read where each tea is a terse stack of brushed name / origin / one line of note, hierarchy coming from brush weight and the breath of negative space rather than boxes — and it *prohibits* photography, rounded cards, gradients, and any color warmth that isn't the paper itself, since the whole thing is one hand and one inkstone. It lands far from the beige hero-photo café template by refusing imagery entirely and trusting an off-center single column on a wide desktop field.
*Risk:* the model may collapse "Japanese + tea" into generic zen-minimalism and lose the specific kissaten grammar of cramped hand-lettering and a single red seal-stamp.

**The reverse of a 1959 Blue Note LP jacket — liner notes set in bold Franklin Gothic**
The owner's sourcing paragraph becomes the liner essay, the three teas become a numbered track listing with tasting notes as parentheticals, and the address/hours/phone drop into the small ruled credits block at the foot — an editorial asymmetry built from one heavy display sans, ruled dividers, and a single spot color. This *prohibits* the symmetric centered "Welcome to Kettle & Leaf" hero, the soft serif, and the card grid, replacing them with a confident left-aligned text block that treats the schedule as fine print.
*Risk:* the jazz signifiers (cover photography, the iconic orange, hard-bop swagger) can pull the design off-brief toward a record-shop identity.

**A page from Vilmorin-Andrieux's 1890 *Les Plantes Potagères*: engraved plate, varietal entries, and a sowing calendar**
Each tea maps to a catalogue varietal entry (name, provenance, descriptive note), the quiet-hours table maps directly onto the sowing/harvest calendar grid, and the Taiwan/Kenya trips become the collector's field note — all set in dense ink-on-buff with one fine line-engraving as the only image. It escapes the generic center by being typographically dense and information-first where the template is sparse and photo-first, and by handling the schedule as a designed almanac table rather than a stray little box.
*Risk:* botanical engraving is already a worn signifier for artisan tea and may read as on-trend rather than escaped, and the form under-specifies the welcome line.

## constraint

**A two-pass letterpress run: warm gray and tea-stain rust, no third ink, no photographs**
Every level of hierarchy must be deduced from overprint, halftone, weight, and rule because only two inks exist, so images are impossible and the page is forced into type-and-line composition with registration overlap as a deliberate feature. This *prohibits* the full-color pour-shot hero and the soft drop-shadowed cards outright, landing well clear of the warm-photo template.
*Risk:* a color constraint under-specifies spatial layout — two ink colors can still be poured into a generic card grid, so it leans on the other slots to fix structure.

**The whole page set as one 380px receipt column, centered in empty space, tear-off rhythm and dotted leaders**
A single narrow measure forces everything to stack — name, welcome, the three teas with dotted leaders running from tea to origin, the quiet hours as right-aligned times, the footer as a totals block — and the surrounding emptiness becomes the design rather than a hero filling the width. This *prohibits* the two-column grid, the full-bleed image, and any horizontal card row, producing a quiet vertical artifact that pointedly refuses the desktop's breadth.
*Risk:* receipt skeuomorphism (perforations, barcode, "thank you, come again") tips quickly into gimmick, and a 380px column can read as squandering the desktop canvas if the emptiness isn't itself composed.

**Nothing set larger than 16px — the shop name is the size of the phone number; rank by rule and space alone**
With type size removed as a tool, importance must be carried entirely by position, white space, rule weight, and grouping, yielding a flat, map-like, almost bureaucratic calm. This *prohibits* the display headline hero and every "big friendly serif" instinct of the template, landing about as far from the cozy center as a transit timetable.
*Risk:* without a size anchor the page can read as undifferentiated directory listing, and the shop's identity and welcome may lack any felt presence.

## metaphor

**The page is a single steeping: pale dry leaf at the top, deepening to full infusion, spent leaf at the foot**
Scroll/read position maps onto extraction time, so the welcome sits pale and faint, the three teas brew at fuller strength, the sourcing paragraph is the deepest steep, and the footer fades to the spent last infusion — tone itself becomes the structural system. It cannot be reskinned for another business because the governing logic *is* tea extraction, and it escapes the flat uniform-warm template by making vertical tone do the ranking.
*Risk:* the read depends on a top-to-bottom tonal gradient that may not survive a single-screen desktop layout, and a continuous gradient can fight a strict two-ink palette.

**The page is a contour map of two hillsides — Pinglin and the Nandi Hills — where every element sits at its elevation**
Each tea is plotted at the altitude it grows, the address is sea-level home, the quiet-hours table becomes the map legend, the sourcing paragraph is the surveyor's note, and contour lines serve as section dividers — geography generates the entire spatial logic. The two named origins make it un-reskinnable and it lands far from the center by replacing decorative warmth with cartographic structure.
*Risk:* it can decay into a literal illustrated map, and it privileges origin over the daily-brew-and-schedule content, under-serving the table and the welcome.

**Kettle versus Leaf: fixed brass apparatus for the standing facts, living seasonal growth for what changes daily**
The page splits into two visual natures — hard, fixed, metallic treatment for the unchanging furniture (name, quiet hours, address, phone) and organic, seasonal, rotating treatment for the living content (today's three teas, the sourcing story) — so the shop's own name becomes the layout's governing dichotomy. Every element must declare which nature it belongs to, which no other subject's page could borrow, and it escapes the uniform-cozy template by deliberately running two registers at once.
*Risk:* two visual languages on one page can fracture into a two-document feel, and the conceit is a near-literal pun on the shop name that could read as cute rather than structural.
