## Overview

Every design decision on this page is deduced from one hard rule.

## Method

Nothing may appear on the page that does not physically exist at a running track. The materials are fixed by the rule: the infield at first light {colors.primary}, the terracotta of the surface itself {colors.tertiary}, aged concrete and worn rubber {colors.secondary}, and the paper of a race programme {colors.neutral}. There is no fifth material, so there is no fifth color. A track has no soft shadows — it lies flat under open sky — so depth on the page comes only from one material meeting another, never from a glow or a drop shadow. A track has no rounded furniture: its geometry is precise arcs, exact distances, and chalk-white right angles, so the page's corners are square and its lines are ruled. The lines themselves are lane markings: thin, exact, low-contrast rules extending across the page the way chalk extends across the oval — structure, not decoration. Type follows the same rule: the engraved authority of a club noticeboard for headlines {typography.headline-lg}, the long-form patience of the programme's prose {typography.body-md}, and the stopwatch's monospace {typography.label-sm} for every number, split, distance, and surface spec — because at a track, data arrives through a timing display. Photography is permitted because the track is itself the subject: full-bleed, documentary, unfiltered. And the terracotta accent obeys the strictest deduction of all: at a real track the start line is painted once per lap, so the accent appears exactly once per panel — the lane marker, not the paint. If an element has no physical counterpart trackside, it does not exist here.

## Colors

The four materials: infield green {colors.primary} for text, surface terracotta {colors.tertiary} once per panel, weathered slate {colors.secondary} for secondary data, programme cream {colors.neutral} as the ground.

## Typography

Noticeboard headlines in {typography.headline-lg}, programme prose in {typography.body-md}, timing-display monospace in {typography.label-sm} for all data.

## Layout

Lane logic: full-width horizontal rules like markings extended onto the page, exact column edges, square corners, generous infield whitespace, one start line of terracotta per panel.
