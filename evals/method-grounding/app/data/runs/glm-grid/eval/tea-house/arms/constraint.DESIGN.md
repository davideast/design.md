## Overview

Every choice on this page is deduced from one fact: it must reproduce as a single two-ink risograph pull on uncoated paper.

## Method

A risograph lays flat soybean ink through a drum onto rough stock {colors.neutral}. It cannot hold a soft shadow—the screen would band and break—so no element on this page casts one; depth is impossible, and the page lies flat on the paper. It cannot saturate a dark field without the ink bleeding through and ghosting the sheet, so there are no dark panels anywhere; large areas stay paper, and structure is made from blank space and the occasional thin rule. The machine gives me exactly two drums. The first is the key, {colors.primary}, carrying all type and every line. Where I want the quieter grey {colors.secondary}—the hours table, the address, a hairline—I do not add a third ink; I under-ink the key drum to a screen, the only grey the press can make.

The second drum is the single warm accent {colors.tertiary}. Because a second pass means re-feeding every sheet and trusting loose registration, it earns its cost only by appearing rarely and never touching the key—one band beneath the shop name, one mark beside today's date, set with enough air that a half-millimetre of misregistration reads as intention rather than error. Fine reversed type would fill in on the rough stock, so all type sits positive, ink on paper, at honest weights. The result is forced, not styled: flat, warm, and plain because the press allows nothing else—and because nothing on the page could survive a second pull that didn't.

## Colors

The two drums: key ink {colors.primary} on paper {colors.neutral}, the key screened back to {colors.secondary} for tables and rules, and the second-drum accent {colors.tertiary} pressed once or twice where it is worth re-feeding the sheet.

## Typography

What the drum holds clean: {typography.headline-lg} for the name and welcome, {typography.body-md} for the teas and the sourcing essay, {typography.label-sm} for the hours table, the address, and the footer—all positive, all flat ink.

## Layout

A single sheet, fed once: wide margins the grippers need, content inset and breathing on open paper, separation built from white space and screened hairlines because that is all a clean pull will keep.
