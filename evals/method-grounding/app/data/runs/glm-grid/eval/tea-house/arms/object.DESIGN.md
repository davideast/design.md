## Overview

This page is not a website for a tea house; it is the reverse of a record jacket that happens to be about tea.

## Method

The model is the back of a 1959 Blue Note LP—Reid Miles's liner side, where bold Franklin Gothic in plain ink crowds a warm off-white field. The shop name and welcome are the album title and its tagline, set largest in {typography.headline-lg}. The three brewed teas are the track listing: each a line with its name set bold, its origin and tasting note trailing in {typography.body-md} the way a personnel credit follows a title. The owner's sourcing paragraph is the liner essay—one columned block of close, confident prose. The quiet-hours table and the address are catalogue data, ranked in {typography.label-sm} like recording dates. Hours and phone are the credits block at the very foot, set off by a single rule.

A Blue Note jacket is square-cornered card stock, so every edge is a true printed corner, the page lying flat on the surface rather than floating above it. We take the *back*, not the front, and we warm it: the field is paper {colors.neutral}, never a dark panel; the ink is {colors.primary}, never reversed out of black. The famous duotone spot is cool blue; ours is a single warm brick {colors.tertiary}, used once or twice—a rule under the title, the lone bullet before today's date—and never as a fill. Depth and shadow have no place on a printed sleeve; the structure is made of white space and the honest weight of the ink.

## Colors

Jacket paper {colors.neutral}, liner ink {colors.primary}, catalogue grey {colors.secondary} for dates and rules, and the warm spot {colors.tertiary} used once at the title and once at the listing.

## Typography

The liner voices: {typography.headline-lg} for the title and welcome, bold-weighted {typography.body-md} for tea names and the essay, {typography.label-sm} for the ruled credits, the hours table, and the catalogue data.

## Layout

A jacket reverse: a wide warm field with firm margins, the title across the top, the track-listing column and the liner essay sharing the middle, and the ruled credits block pinned along the foot.
