## Overview

This publication is a printed annual, not a fitness website.

## Method

This is the centenary annual of an athletics club that has been meeting on the same oval since 1923 — crossed with the race programme sold for a shilling at the gate in 1948. Heavy cream stock {colors.neutral}, the club's deep green {colors.primary} for every word of text, photography printed full-page like commemorative plates, and results set the way a timekeeper sets them: monospace columns {typography.label-sm} of names, distances, surfaces, and splits, every figure earned. The voice is the club historian's — proud, exact, unhurried, writing for members who will keep the volume on a shelf for fifty years. Such an annual is laid out like a book: headlines ranged left in the masthead serif {typography.headline-lg}, prose at a reading measure {typography.body-md}, captions and data in small capitals, photographs given whole pages out of respect rather than cropped into a grid. Nothing on its pages shines, floats, or rounds: it is ink pressed into paper, right angles and rules, the geometry of lanes. The one color the printer adds beyond green is the orange-red of the track surface itself {colors.tertiary} — spent once per spread, on the single thing the reader is meant to do or feel, the way a programme circles the feature race.

Two departures from the bound original: the photography is modern and cinematic — dawn light, mid-stride motion — printed at full bleed as the plates of this edition; and the timekeeper's monospace does double duty on screen as eyebrow labels and map annotations, the data panel beside each course.

## Colors

Programme cream {colors.neutral}, club green {colors.primary} for all text, weathered slate {colors.secondary} for captions and metadata, and track terracotta {colors.tertiary} — the feature-race circle, once per panel.

## Typography

The annual's voices: {typography.headline-lg} for headlines ranged left, {typography.body-md} for the historian's prose, {typography.label-sm} for results, specs, and eyebrows — the timekeeper's column.

## Layout

Book pages at screen scale: full-page plates alternating with text spreads, generous margins, rules where a results table needs them, each panel composed like a page meant to be kept.
