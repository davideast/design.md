Track Nº 01 Plate I
Georgetown Waterfront Track

A riverside oval kept honest by the Potomac wind.

Built on landfill where the C&O Canal meets the river, the Georgetown oval is the city's most exposed circuit. The wind comes off the water unbroken, and on a March morning it will take a full second from your 400. The surface, laid in 1998, has cured to a colour closer to dust than terracotta — soft underfoot in the rain, fast and true in the dry.

Regulars know the back straight by the boathouse lights switching off at dawn. You do not run here for shelter. You run here because the river makes the lap feel earned.

Plate III — Anacostia Park, 06:14 EDT
Track Nº 02 Plate III
Anacostia Park Track

An east-of-the-river oval, generous and unhurried.

Anacostia is the longer circuit, and you feel the extra metres in the calves by the third interval. The surface is older than Georgetown's — a pour from the late eighties, patched twice, still holding the impression of a thousand November cross-country finishes. Trees line the home straight; in October the track is carpeted in sycamore.

This is where the club's older members come to run alone. There is no wind off the water here. There is shade, and the long quiet of a Sunday that has not yet begun.
