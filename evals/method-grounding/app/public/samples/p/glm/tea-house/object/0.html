A Note from the Owner — Liner Essay

Twice a year I pack a duffel, a tasting set, and a notebook the size of a paperback, and I leave Maple Row for the hills where the leaves actually grow. In Taiwan that means the narrow switchbacks above Beipu and the older gardens outside Nantou, where the farmers I buy from have been finishing oolong the same way for three generations.

In Kenya it means a longer flight and a dustier road, climbing west toward Kericho, where smallholder plots sit at the cloud line and the purple-leaf cultivar was first coaxed into a black tea worth drinking on its own. I cup what they've dried, I argue gently about price, and I carry twenty kilos home in a hard-shell case that the customs officers have learned to expect.

Every tea on the board above the counter began as a handshake on a porch somewhere. That is the part of the work I love most, and the part you taste first.
