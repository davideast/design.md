§ 1. The Loop

At its core, the ReAct (Reasoning and Acting) loop acknowledges that language models are most effective when they externalize their internal state. Rather than attempting to compute a complex multi-step trajectory in a single forward pass, the model is constrained to a cyclical protocol. It first generates a Thought—a natural language representation of its current understanding and next intent. It then performs an Action by calling an external tool. Finally, it receives an Observation from the environment. This triplet repeats. The loop transforms the model from a stochastic parrot into a feedback-driven controller, capable of error correction and tool-augmented reasoning.

Fig. 1. The basic loop. Note that OBSERVE returns to THINK; this is what makes the architecture a controller, not a pipeline.

§ 2. A Worked Trace
Query: "What was the closing price of NVDA the day after Jensen Huang's 2024 keynote?"
1. THOUGHT: Need to identify the date of Jensen Huang's 2024 keynote.
2. ACTION: search("Jensen Huang 2024 keynote date")
3. OBSERVE: "Jensen Huang delivered the GTC 2024 keynote on March 18, 2024."
4. THOUGHT: Next day is March 19, 2024. Need NVDA closing price for that date.
5. ACTION: search("NVDA closing price March 19 2024")
6. OBSERVE: "On March 19, 2024, NVDA closed at $893.98."
7. THOUGHT: I have the price for the day after the keynote. Ready to answer.
8. ACTION: finish("$893.98")
Initial discovery phase.
Temporal grounding of the query.
Specific data retrieval.
Termination condition met.

§ 4. Where Loops Fail

Agentic loops are not free. Every iteration of the Think-Act-Observe cycle appends the previous steps to the model’s context window. As the trajectory grows, the prompt becomes increasingly dense with historical observations and failed attempts. Eventually, the model enters a regime of context exhaustion. Performance degrades sharply once the token count crosses the threshold where the model’s effective attention can no longer reliably distinguish relevant history from noise, leading to repetitive loops or hallucinated observations.

Most production agents fail somewhere in this region — not because the model is incapable, but because the context has become longer than the model's effective attention.

§ 5. The Loop, in Code
def agent_loop(query): context = [SystemMessage(PROMPT), UserMessage(query)] while True: # Generate next step response = model.generate(context) thought, action = parse(response) print(f"Thought: {thought}") if action.name == "finish": return action.output # Execute and observe observation = tools[action.name](action.args) print(f"Observation: {observation}") # Update context window context.append(AssistantMessage(response)) context.append(UserMessage(f"Obs: {observation}"))
THINK: The model externalizes state.
ACT: Side-effects occur here.
OBSERVE: Environment feeds back.

The structure strictly interleaves reasoning and action. Forcing the model to externalize its thought before acting gives the system interpretability and reduces hallucinated state changes.

§ 6. Ending

The architecture is the loop. Everything else is a refinement of how the loop decides what to do next.
