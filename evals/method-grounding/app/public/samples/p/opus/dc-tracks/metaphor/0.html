The Tracks of Washington DC

A field guide to the city's ovals — their surfaces, their histories, their regulars.

Georgetown Waterfront Track

A blue rectangle held against the river's slow turn.

Tucked beneath the spires of the university, the waterfront oval runs cooler than the rest of the city — fed by the Potomac's morning damp and shaded long into the afternoon. The surface gives slightly, then returns, the way a good lane should. Crews come up from the boathouse to log their land miles here, and the regulars know the exact corner where the wind first finds you.

Distance 400 M
Surface POLYURETHANE
Ward WARD 2
The oval teaches that there is no shortcut around the bend — only the willingness to take it again.
Anacostia Park Track

Worn terracotta beneath the flight path, open to the whole sky.

East of the river, the Anacostia oval sits in the broadest infield in the city — flat, generous, unhurried. The terracotta has faded to the colour of old brick, and the lane chalk is renewed by hand each spring. High-school relay squads and shift-workers off the night share its lanes at the strange hours, and the planes overhead keep a rhythm of their own.

Distance 400 M
Surface WEATHERED CLAY
Ward WARD 8
